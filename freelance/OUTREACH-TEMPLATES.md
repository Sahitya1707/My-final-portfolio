# Outreach email templates

Replace everything in `[brackets]`. Every email must contain at least one observation that is **true and specific to that business**: something you checked yourself on their site, on your phone. If you can't find one, don't email them.

Keep it under ~120 words. Plain text, no images, no attachments, one link to your site and at most one project link.

---

## 1. First email: business with an outdated or hard-to-use website

**Subject options** (pick one, keep it plain):
- `Quick note about [business name]'s website`
- `[Business name] on mobile`
- `Your menu page`  *(or whatever the specific thing is)*

```
Hi [first name, or "there" if you can't find one],

I was looking at [business name]'s website on my phone and noticed [specific
observation, e.g. "the menu is a PDF that opens tiny and has to be zoomed to read"
/ "the phone number isn't tappable, so calling means copying it by hand"].

For [type of customer, e.g. "someone deciding where to eat tonight"], that's often
the moment they give up and pick somewhere else.

I'm an independent web developer in [town/region]. I'd fix this by [one concrete
change, e.g. "putting the menu on a normal mobile page with a one-tap call button"],
and I usually keep my clients' hosting costs at or near zero.

Here's some of my work: [your site URL]/work/

Would a 15-minute call next week be useful? Or if you'd prefer, I can send three
or four quick notes on what I'd change, no charge.

[Your name]
[Your site URL] · [phone]
[City, Province]

If you'd rather not hear from me, just reply "no thanks" and I won't email again.
```

## 2. First email: business with no website

```
Subject: A website for [business name]?

Hi [first name],

I came across [business name] on [Google Maps / Facebook / Instagram]. [Specific
genuine detail: "Your reviews mention the butter tarts a lot" / "Your photos of the
patio work are great"], but I couldn't find a website, so the Google listing is doing
all the work.

A simple site with your [services / menu / hours], photos and a one-tap call button
would give people somewhere to land when they search for you. I build these for small
businesses, and the hosting can cost nothing per month; you'd only pay for the domain
name (about $20 a year).

Some recent work: [your site URL]/work/

Open to a quick chat about it?

[Your name]
[Your site URL] · [phone]
[City, Province]

Reply "no thanks" and I won't contact you again.
```

## 3. First email: startup or individual needing an app

```
Subject: [Product/company name], and an extra pair of hands

Hi [first name],

I saw [where: their launch post / job ad / Product Hunt page] about [product]. [One
specific, genuine observation about the product or a problem they mentioned.]

I'm an independent developer working with React, Next.js, Node.js and .NET. I build
products in small fixed-price stages so you see working software early. I'm currently
building my own Android app (Roadstar) end to end, so I understand shipping with a small
team: [your site URL]/work/roadstar/

If you need help with [specific thing], I'd be glad to talk it through.

[Your name]
[Your site URL]

Not relevant? Reply "no thanks" and I won't follow up.
```

## 4. Follow-up 1 (4–5 days later, reply in the same thread)

```
Hi [first name],

Just bumping this up in case it got buried. Happy to send those few notes on the
[site / menu page / mobile layout], no strings attached, if that's easier than a call.

[Your name]
```

## 5. Follow-up 2 (10–14 days after the first email, final)

```
Hi [first name],

I'll leave it here so I'm not filling your inbox. If the website ever moves up the
list, I'm at [email] and [your site URL].

Good luck with [something specific: the new location / the busy season / the launch].

[Your name]
```

Then mark the prospect `lost` in your tracker (you can revisit in six months if they never opted out). Don't email a third time.

## 6. When they say "send the notes"

```
Hi [first name],

Thanks! Here's what I'd look at first, in order of impact:

1. [Most important fix, in customer terms, e.g. "Make the phone number tappable on
   every page. Most of your visitors are on phones and calling is what you want."]
2. [Second fix]
3. [Third fix]
4. [Optional: speed, e.g. "The home page takes about 6 seconds to load on mobile
   data (Google's PageSpeed test). Compressing the large photos would roughly halve that."]

You or whoever manages the site can do these yourselves. If you'd like me to handle
it, I'd quote [fixed price range or "a fixed price"] for [scope]. Either way, I hope
it's useful.

[Your name]
```

## 7. When they ask "how much?"

```
Hi [first name],

Good question. It depends mostly on the number of pages and whether you need anything
beyond a contact form (bookings, online ordering, etc.). For a site like yours I'd
expect it to be in the range of [range from PLAYBOOK.md], as a fixed price agreed in
writing before I start. Hosting can be $0 a month; you'd just pay for the domain.

The easiest way to get you an exact number is a 15-minute call. Does [day] or [day]
work?

[Your name]
```

---

## Checklist before you press send

- [ ] Business email is published by the business itself, and nothing says "no solicitations"
- [ ] At least one specific, true observation you personally checked
- [ ] No exaggeration, no fake urgency, no criticism of whoever built their site
- [ ] Your name, site, phone or address, and the opt-out line are in the footer
- [ ] The project you link is relevant to their type of business
- [ ] Logged in the tracker with a next-action date
