# Freelance playbook

A practical starting plan for finding your first website clients with $0 of spend. Written for where you are now: real skills and real projects, no paying clients yet. Revisit the numbers after your first three projects.

---

## 1. Positioning: one sentence

> **I design and build fast, clear websites for small local businesses, and I keep their running costs close to zero.**

Why this works for you:

- **Small local businesses** are reachable by email and walking distance, rarely have an in-house developer, and are under-served by agencies that start at $8–10k.
- **"Fast, clear"** is something you can demonstrate on their own phone in 30 seconds.
- **"Running costs close to zero"** is a real, rare advantage. Many small businesses pay $30–60 a month for Wix/Squarespace/hosting they don't need. You can show them a site on free hosting.

What you are **not** (yet): a branding agency, an SEO agency, a marketing company. Don't promise rankings, leads or revenue.

### Pick a starting niche (recommended)

Generalists get ignored in cold email. Pick one or two types of business for your first 50 emails so your examples and observations are sharper. Good first niches near Barrie / Richmond Hill / the GTA:

- Restaurants, cafés and bakeries (menus as PDFs, no mobile layout, no click-to-call)
- Trades: plumbers, electricians, landscapers, cleaners (often no site or a 2012 site; phone calls are the conversion)
- Salons, barbers, physio and wellness clinics (booking links buried)
- Community groups and non-profits (your Elmvale project is directly relevant)

## 2. Services and starting prices

Prices in CAD, set for a new independent developer in Ontario: below agency rates, high enough to be taken seriously and to be worth your time. Quote fixed prices, not hours, for anything you can scope.

| Service | What's included | Starting price | Typical time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Landing page** | 1 page, mobile-first, contact/booking form, launch on free hosting | CA$500 – 900 | ~1 week |
| **Small business website** | Up to ~6 pages, contact form, map, click-to-call, basic SEO setup, free hosting, 30 days of fixes | CA$1,200 – 2,500 | 2–4 weeks |
| **Redesign** | Written review of the current site, rebuild keeping content and URLs, redirects | CA$1,500 – 3,000 | 3–5 weeks |
| **Web application** | Scoped in stages; each stage fixed-price | From CA$4,000, or CA$55–75/hour for open-ended work | Varies |
| **Maintenance** | Updates, fixes, small changes, check-ups | CA$60/hour, or CA$100–200/month for a set number of hours | Ongoing |
| **Quick fixes** | Speed, mobile layout, broken forms on an existing site | CA$150 – 500 fixed | Days |

**How to quote:**

1. After the first conversation, write a one-page proposal: goal, pages/features, what's *not* included, timeline, price, payment terms.
2. Payment: **50% to start, 50% before launch** (or 40/30/30 for bigger jobs). Use e-Transfer or a free invoice from Wave/Stripe.
3. Include **two rounds of revisions** per stage; extra changes are quoted separately.
4. The client pays for their own domain (and owns it). You set up free hosting under an account they own, or transfer it on completion.

**Your first two or three projects ("founding clients"):** offer about 30–40% off in exchange for (a) permission to feature the work with screenshots and (b) an honest testimonial if they're happy. Say so openly; don't drop to $0 except for a cause you genuinely want to support. After three, return to the table above.

**Raise prices when** you're booked more than a month out, or more than half of quotes are accepted without pushback.

## 3. Finding prospects (free)

1. **Google Maps**: search "bakery Barrie", "plumber Newmarket" etc. Open each listing: website missing, broken, not mobile-friendly, or obviously dated? That's a prospect.
2. Check their site on **your phone** and with <https://pagespeed.web.dev> (free). Note 2–3 specific, verifiable problems.
3. Find a **published business email** on their site, Google listing or Facebook page. Only use addresses the business itself publishes. **Never buy lists or scrape personal emails.**
4. Log them in your tracker (section 6) *before* emailing.

Aim for quality: **5–10 personalised emails a day**, not 100 generic ones. A realistic reply rate for good personalised cold email is a few percent; expect to send 50–100 to get a handful of conversations. That's normal, not failure.

## 4. Staying legal and decent

**Canada (CASL).** Cold commercial email is allowed under *implied consent* only when the address is **conspicuously published** by the business, it **doesn't say "no unsolicited messages"**, and your message is **relevant to their business role** (a website offer to a business is). Every email must:

- say who you are and include your mailing address or phone and email,
- include a simple way to opt out ("Reply 'no thanks' and I won't contact you again"), honoured within 10 days,
- not be misleading in the subject or content.

**US (CAN-SPAM)** is similar: honest subject, physical address, opt-out. Other countries may be stricter; stick to Canada and the US to start.

Also:

- Never pretend you're already working with them, or that their site "has been flagged". No fake urgency.
- Never mock their current site. The person reading may have built it, or paid a nephew to.
- Send from your own address, one at a time. No mail-merge blasts.
- Two follow-ups maximum, then stop.

## 5. The outreach approach

Templates are in `OUTREACH-TEMPLATES.md`. The structure:

1. **Specific observation** about *their* site, from real checking (one sentence).
2. **Why it matters** to their customers (one sentence).
3. **What you'd do** about it (one or two sentences).
4. **Proof**: one link to the most relevant project, plus your site.
5. **Low-pressure ask**: a 15-minute call, or "want me to send a few quick notes?"

The strongest offer you can make early on is a **free, short written review** (5 bullet points, 15 minutes of your time) or a **quick visual mock-up of their home page** for businesses you're especially keen on. It proves you looked and gives value even if they say no.

## 6. Tracking prospects for free

Use `prospects-template.csv`: import it into **Google Sheets** (File → Import) or open it in Excel/Numbers.

| Column | Use |
|---|---|
| `status` | `to-contact` → `emailed` → `followed-up-1` → `followed-up-2` → `replied` → `call-booked` → `proposal-sent` → `won` / `lost` / `opted-out` |
| `next_action_date` | Sort by this every morning |
| `observations` | The specific issues you found; reuse in your email |
| `opt_out` | `yes` means never contact again. Keep the row so you don't re-add them. |

Follow-up rhythm: first email day 0 → follow-up 1 on day 4–5 → follow-up 2 on day 10–14 → close the loop.

Weekly review (20 minutes, Friday): emails sent, replies, calls, proposals, wins. Change the niche or the opening line if replies stay at zero after ~40 emails.

## 7. When someone replies

1. Reply within a few hours if you can. Speed is a competitive advantage.
2. Book a 15–20 minute call (Google Meet/Zoom free tier, or phone).
3. On the call ask: what does the business do, who are the customers, how do customers find you today, what should the website make them do (call, book, visit, order)? Is there a deadline? A rough budget?
4. Send the one-page proposal within two working days.
5. Once they accept: deposit first, then start. Send short weekly updates with the preview link.

## 8. After each project

- Add it to `content/projects.ts` (or ask Claude Code with `/add-project`).
- Ask for a testimonial **two weeks after launch** with three easy prompts: what was the problem, what was it like working with you, what's different now.
- Offer the maintenance plan.
- Ask: "Do you know another business owner who'd find this useful?" Referrals will soon beat cold email.
